01 — Boreal Forest · British Columbia

Ancient
Forest
Endangered

Canada's boreal forest is the world's largest intact forest ecosystem — a vast, ancient wilderness spanning over 550 million hectares. Yet each year, illegal dumping and industrial encroachment push its boundaries further. These old-growth giants filter our water, store our carbon, and shelter thousands of species found nowhere else on Earth.

550M hectares of boreal forest
3.4M tonnes of waste dumped annually

02 — Atlantic & Pacific · Coastal Waters

Two
Oceans.
One Crisis.

From the rugged shores of Newfoundland to the kelp forests of British Columbia, Canada's coastlines stretch over 243,000 kilometres — the longest in the world. Microplastics now permeate every depth. Tidal currents carry the weight of human carelessness into waters that have remained pristine for millennia.

243K km of coastline — world's longest
82% of shoreline surveys find plastic

03 — Prairies · Saskatchewan · Alberta

The
Prairie
Horizon

Canada's native grasslands once stretched as far as the eye could see — a living, breathing tapestry of thousands of plant species, below which lies some of Earth's deepest and most fertile soil. Today, less than 20% of this biome remains intact. The prairie is not empty land; it is an irreplaceable archive of biodiversity.

<20% of native grassland remains
70B tonnes of carbon stored in soil
